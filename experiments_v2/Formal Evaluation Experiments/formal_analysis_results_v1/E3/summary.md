# E3 — Planning and feedback safety decomposition

Population: 360 retained attempts, 348 scientific-complete and 12 infrastructure failures. All attempts remain in operational accounting; frozen physical metrics have explicit valid and NA counts.

Controlling feedback, P1 increased predicted minimum separation by 1.274 m (95% CI 1.135 to 1.413; paired dz=1.348), reduced realized hard-risk events by 1.604 per attempt, and reduced hard-risk exposure by 1.300 pair-s. Those planning effects are concentrated in the registered predictable-structural Family A and mixed Family C; Family B has intentionally safe nominal plans and shows no planning effect. Under F1, P1 also reduced feedback activation burden by 5.747 UAV-s.

Controlling planning, F1 did not reduce frozen realized-risk endpoints: the pooled hard-risk-exposure difference was 0.015 pair-s (95% CI -0.119 to 0.149) and the event-count difference was 0.030. Family B likewise showed no reduction. F1 improved all-attempt mission success by 5.6 percentage points, but that operational result does not establish the proposed residual-risk responsibility.

Verdict: `C2_PLANNING_EXECUTION_DECOMPOSITION: PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`.

Maximum defensible wording: In the evaluated UAV-swarm reconfiguration scenarios, safety-aware planning reduced predictable nominal conflict, realized hard-risk exposure, and the corrective burden left to feedback in the registered structural- and mixed-risk families. The frozen experiment did not show that reactive feedback reduced residual realized hard-risk exposure in the registered residual-risk family, so the full planning-execution responsibility decomposition is only partially supported.
